Wynn / Encore

Poker room: Wynn Poker Room (WPT World Championship host room)

Checked 2026-11-19: The official room page and all three extracted chunks of the daily structures were read November 19. Four recurring daily formats publish entry fees and guarantees; all permit extra spending. Exact-date availability during series remains unverified.

Wynn's official WPT World Championship structures PDF prints 'Unlimited re-entry until the start of level 9' for the $600 Turbo, the $600 mixed-game events, and the $400 / $1,100 PLO events.
cdn.wynnresorts.com, retrieved 2026-11-16
The same PDF prints the buy-in splits, e.g. $600 NLH $400K GTD is $510 prize pool + $65 house fee + $25 staff gratuity.
cdn.wynnresorts.com, retrieved 2026-11-16
Daily structures: $200 Monday–Thursday noon, $240 Friday noon rebuy/add-on, $200 weekend noon, $160 nightly 6 p.m. Friday prohibits re-entry but allows rebuys and an add-on; the other three allow re-entry.
cdn.wynnresorts.com, retrieved 2026-11-19

Verdict: Excluded from the single-entry ranking. Two independent official statements support the exclusion: the WPT structures PDF prints unlimited re-entry on named events, and the room's own page advertises the Friday daily as a '$40,000 guaranteed NLH Rebuy' - a rebuy is a second buy-in, so the event cannot enter a single-entry table on either reading.

Provenance: Daily page and structures read first-hand 2026-11-19; earlier WPT readings retain their original dates.. Official page: www.wynnlasvegas.com

Caesars Palace

Poker room: Caesars Palace Poker Room

Checked 2026-11-17: Read first-hand on 2026-11-17 on Caesars' own poker page. It does publish a daily tournament slate for Caesars Palace: 'four No-Limit Hold'em tournaments each day at 10 a.m., 2 p.m., 6 p.m. and 9 p.m. Every event carries a $100 buy-in, starts you with 20,000 chips, and features a $1,000 guaranteed prize pool. Blinds increase every 20 minutes ... and a $50 add-on at the first break gives you an extra 15,000 chips'. That is the first time this site has an official buy-in, stack, guarantee and level length for a Caesars daily; it is still not a structure sheet, and the add-on is decisive. The property's own daily flyer PDF, read the same day (session arena/7cc9ee65-poker2), gives the same four start times and the same $1,000 guarantee but prices them $100 / $160 / $100 / $160 - see F-14.

Caesars' corporate disclaimer publishes only a resort-fee range: 'a daily resort fee between $45-$50 plus applicable tax. Resort fees are waived for Diamond and Seven Stars.' No single property value is published.
www.caesars.com, retrieved 2026-11-16
Read live 2026-11-17 from the official daily flyer: 'All Tournaments feature a $50 add-on for 15,000 tournament chips that can only be taken at the first break. Registration will close at the start of level seven.'
www.caesars.com, retrieved 2026-11-17
Read live 2026-11-17: '3. Participants may only register themselves, but may re-enter, by paying the entry fee, upon elimination within the first break of play.'
www.caesars.com, retrieved 2026-11-17
Read live 2026-11-17: '6. Of the $100 buy in, $70 goes to the prize pool and $30 is held by Caesars Palace as a maintenance fee.' and '7. Of the $160 buy in, $115 goes to the prize pool and $45 is held by Caesars Palace as a maintenance fee.'

Verdict: Excluded from the single-entry table on the operator's own words: the published $50 add-on is a second purchase of chips, which is exactly the money-buys-an-advantage case the brief puts in a separate section. It can be listed and priced, but not compared like-for-like with the sheet-silent Venetian candidates, and the buy-in conflicts between Caesars' own page ($100 for all four) and its own daily flyer ($160 at 2 p.m. and 9 p.m.) - see F-14.
