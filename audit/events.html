The single-entry question, answered plainly

The brief asks for single-entry, freezeout-confirmed tournaments as the main section. That cannot be built from these documents, and the reason is specific rather than a technicality:

  • 0 rows are confirmed single-entry. Not one of the 37 sheets prints a freezeout, no-rebuy or no-add-on rule.
  • 22 rows print an entry restriction, and every one of them is the same flight rule: “Any player who bags chips for Day 2 may not play a subsequent flight.” That stops a player who has already survived a flight from buying another one. It says nothing about a player who busted, and allowing exactly that is the point of a multi-flight event. It is not a freezeout clause and the site does not label it as one.
  • 72 rows are on a sheet that was read in full and prints no entry limit at all. Silence is recorded as silence.

So the two sections below are split by structure, which the documents do establish, not by an entry rule the documents do not establish. Every row shows its own Entry rule cell with the quoted evidence, so a reader can judge for themselves. Full reasoning: FD-03. The question that would close it: IQ-03.

How the two sections differ

The first section is one-day events: you buy in once, play one flight, and the event finishes that day or runs to a Day 2 you have already qualified for. The second section is everything where the buy-in buys something structurally different — a flight of a multi-flight event, a satellite whose prize is a seat rather than money, or a team entry. Those are not comparable with the first section on price or on guarantee, so they are not mixed in.
