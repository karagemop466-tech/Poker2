The information that must exist before a booking decision

Questions

Twelve questions, each traceable to a specific missing value in the data rather than a generic worry. 5 of them block a decision: until they are answered, the only honest recommendation is not to book. The rest change the shape of the trip rather than its viability.

12

The blocking questions, at a glance

  • Q-01 — What are the exact trip dates and the number of nights? Nothing in the repo fixes a stay window. Room totals, walk-day routing and event choices are all date-specific.
  • Q-02 — Is a maximum of one entry per event actually required, and would the player accept a re-entry event if it were cheap? Whether the user's 'single entry only' rule is hard (never play a re-entry event) or soft (prefer single-entry, accept a capped event).
  • Q-03 — What is the maximum total buy-in per day and for the trip? No bankroll or per-day cap was supplied, so the ranking cannot apply a cost ceiling.
  • Q-04 — What hotel quality floor is acceptable, and is the poker rate (which requires 6 hours of play per night) acceptable? Whether the player can meet the six-hour condition on every night, and whether a 4-star or 5-star floor is required.
  • Q-05 — Is a 10:00 AM start acceptable, given that no 8 AM tournament exists? Whether the 8 AM requirement is a hard constraint.

Before you book a room

blocking Q-01 — What are the exact trip dates and the number of nights?

What the official sources do say. The verified Strip calendar runs Nov 16 – Dec 23, 2026 (Venetian DSE IV) and Nov 27 – Dec 21 (Wynn WPT).

What is missing. Nothing in the repo fixes a stay window. Room totals, walk-day routing and event choices are all date-specific.

How to close it. Pick a 7-night window inside Nov 25 – Dec 23, 2026 (the only window where two properties both publish schedules).

What it changes. Hotel cost, trip cost, day plan

blocking Q-03 — What is the maximum total buy-in per day and for the trip?

What the official sources do say. Verified buy-ins in the window range from $100 (Caesars Palace daily) to $25,800 (Wynn High Roller).

What is missing. No bankroll or per-day cap was supplied, so the ranking cannot apply a cost ceiling.

How to close it. State a per-day and a trip maximum.

What it changes. Ranking, day plan, trip cost

blocking Q-04 — What hotel quality floor is acceptable, and is the poker rate (which requires 6 hours of play per night) acceptable?

What the official sources do say. Venetian publishes a poker rate from $119/$209 that requires six hours of live cash play or a $300+ tournament buy-in per night, or the rate reverts to the prevailing rate.

What is missing. Whether the player can meet the six-hour condition on every night, and whether a 4-star or 5-star floor is required.

How to close it. Choose a star floor and decide whether to book the poker rate or the public rate.

What it changes. True nightly cost, hotel ranking

medium Q-09 — What does a room actually cost for the chosen dates, all-in?

What the official sources do say. Resort fees are $50–$55 plus tax at every property that publishes one; the Clark County room tax is 13.38% on the Venetian page.

What is missing. Every dated room rate. Booking engines return placeholders to a non-browser client.

How to close it. Capture a checkout total in a browser for the chosen dates and occupancy, and record the rate timestamp.

What it changes. True nightly cost, trip cost

medium Q-10 — Do the walk times hold at the actual times of day, and from the actual hotel tower?

What the official sources do say. Google Maps walking times were measured for 14 hotel→room pairs on 2026-11-16.

What is missing. Times are point-to-point and measured at one moment; they exclude in-building walking and do not account for crowd levels.

How to close it. Add 5–10 minutes per walk and re-measure on arrival.

What it changes. Hotel shortlist, day plan

Before you trust a ranking

blocking Q-02 — Is a maximum of one entry per event actually required, and would the player accept a re-entry event if it were cheap?

What the official sources do say. Zero events are confirmed single-entry. Every property that states a rule states re-entry, rebuy or a paid add-on.

What is missing. Whether the user's 'single entry only' rule is hard (never play a re-entry event) or soft (prefer single-entry, accept a capped event).

How to close it. Answer the constraint question. If hard, the dataset yields no bookable event and the trip is not possible on official information.

What it changes. Everything on the ranking page

high Q-06 — Does the Venetian/Venetian-Palazzo poker room publish a maximum-entry rule anywhere not yet read?

What the official sources do say. 14 of 37 DSE IV structure sheets were read in full and none states an entry cap.

What is missing. 23 structure sheets unread; no Venetian tournament-rules page could be found (the /tournament-rules.html path returns 404).

How to close it. Read the remaining 23 sheets, or ask lv_cas_poker@venetianlasvegas.com in writing.

What it changes. Whether any event can ever be marked single-entry

high Q-07 — What is the all-in entry cost per event, including staff gratuities that buy chips?

What the official sources do say. Fee splits are verified for 14 Venetian sheets, 6 Wynn WPT events, all four Caesars Palace dailies, all four Wynn dailies and both Bellagio weeklies.

What is missing. Several Bellagio and MGM Grand events attach a *voluntary* gratuity that buys extra chips. Whether to count that as part of the entry cost is an open modelling choice, since not paying it puts you at a chip disadvantage.

How to close it. Decide whether 'entry cost' means the mandatory minimum or the cost of a competitive stack (mandatory + gratuity).

What it changes. True cost per event, ranking ratio

low Q-12 — Does the Bellagio and MGM Grand 'voluntary' gratuity change the effective rake?

What the official sources do say. Bellagio $40 gratuity buys +15,000 (Fri) or +20,000 (Sat) chips on top of 30,000/40,000. MGM Grand $11 buys +10,000 on top of 15,000.

What is missing. Whether declining the gratuity is realistic in a competitive field.

How to close it. Model entry cost twice: minimum and with-gratuity.

What it changes. Cost per event

Before you commit to a schedule

blocking Q-05 — Is a 10:00 AM start acceptable, given that no 8 AM tournament exists?

What the official sources do say. Earliest verified start is 10:00 AM (Caesars Palace) / 10:10 AM (Venetian). Horseshoe shows 9:00 AM on an editorial page only.

What is missing. Whether the 8 AM requirement is a hard constraint.

How to close it. Accept 10 AM, or verify Horseshoe's 9 AM slot directly with the room.

What it changes. Day plan feasibility

medium Q-11 — Which event should be the primary on each day, given that a long 1-day event blocks an evening event?

What the official sources do say. Venetian 1-day structures run up to 28 levels at 30 minutes plus seven 15-minute breaks, i.e. a derived ceiling of 15 h 45 min from the 11:10 AM start.

What is missing. Actual finish times and field sizes.

How to close it. Treat the day plan's 'conditional fallback' as contingent on a bust before the fallback's late-registration close, and check the close time on the day.

What it changes. Day plan

Before you decide anything else

high Q-08 — Is the published guarantee actually reachable in the field sizes these events draw?

What the official sources do say. Guaranteed pool and prize-pool-per-entry are both known for most events, so the number of entries needed to fund the guarantee can be computed exactly.

What is missing. Actual field sizes are not published before the event. No historical attendance was retrieved.

How to close it. Use the computed 'entries needed to fund the guarantee' as the comparison, and check the property's published results pages for past field sizes.

What it changes. Guarantee ÷ entry ratio interpretation

How to ask

Every question above can be answered by one of four routes, in descending order of authority: (1) the property's poker-room email or phone — the Venetian publishes lv_cas_poker@venetianlasvegas.com and 702.414.7657, the Wynn publishes poker.reservations@wynnlasvegas.com and 702-770-7654, Bellagio publishes pcohen@bellagioresort.com and 702.693.7291, MGM Grand publishes 702.891.7434; (2) a named tournament director's written statement; (3) the property's own booking flow, screenshot with timestamp; (4) a third-party aggregator — which is how most of the existing web answers to these questions were produced, and why they cannot be used as evidence here.

When an answer arrives, record it in this repository with the name of the person who gave it, the date, and the channel. An email is a citable source; a phone call is a citable source if you note who you spoke to.
