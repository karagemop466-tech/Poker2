How the ratio works — and what it is not

Guarantee ÷ entry fee answers one question: how many full buy-ins the advertised guarantee represents. A ratio of 100 means the room has advertised a prize pool equal to 100 buy-ins. That is a description of the advertised number, nothing else.

It is not a forecast, an expected return, an overlay estimate, or a statement that the event is +EV. Three reasons it cannot be read that way: (1) the guarantee is a floor the room promises, not the amount of money in the prize pool — the pool is the actual entries × prize-pool-per-entry; (2) the room keeps the fee, so the ratio is not a return on your buy-in; (3) field size is unknown before the event.

The column that makes the ratio interpretable is entries needed to fund the guarantee — guarantee ÷ prize-pool-per-entry, straight from the structure sheet. If an event needs 1,000 entries to fund its guarantee and the room draws 200, the room is paying an overlay; if it needs 60, the guarantee is a formality. Both are useful, neither is a forecast.

Why no event is ranked as a confirmed single-entry event

Of the 37 Venetian DeepStack structure sheets linked from the official schedule, 14 were opened and read in full for this snapshot. Every one of them sets the buy-in, the fee split, the starting stack, the level length and the late-registration close. None of them states a maximum number of entries. None of them mentions an add-on. Several of them explicitly restrict play after a player bags chips for day 2 — which proves the sheets do know how to write an entry restriction when one exists.

That is the strongest finding in this dataset, and it cuts both ways: it is the best available evidence that these could be one-entry events, and it is not evidence that they are. A written confirmation from the room is the only thing that converts a blank into a yes.

Every other property publishes the opposite. Wynn says "Unlimited re-entry for each starting flight … until the start of level 9". Caesars Palace says players "may re-enter, by paying the entry fee, upon elimination within the first break of play" and sells a $50 add-on. Bellagio says "Players may buy-in and re-enter through the end of level 8".
