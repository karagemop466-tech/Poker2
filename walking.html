Read this before relying on a number

These are point-to-point times between Google's property pins. They are not published by any casino, they exclude the walk from your room to the lobby, and they do not account for crowd levels on a weekend night. Google's pin for a large resort is not necessarily its lobby door.

Practical rule: add 5–10 minutes to every figure below for the in-building walk, and treat anything at 25 minutes or more as over the limit. The Bellagio→Venetian pair is the clearest example: its best route measures 28 minutes and an alternative route measures 30, so it is on the edge and should not be treated as a comfortable walk.

The strongest objection to this page, stated fairly

A parallel session working on the same brief refused to apply the 30-minute cap at all, on the grounds that no casino publishes a walking duration, distance or route between a Strip hotel and a poker room — so any number you put in the table would be an invention wearing a number's clothes. That session recorded the constraint as blocked rather than measured, and its reasoning is sound: a routing service is not a property policy.

Why this page measures anyway. The brief requires a walking-route check with a 30-minute limit. Refusing to measure leaves the requirement unanswered; measuring with the source class stated gives you something you can check yourself in thirty seconds by opening the same route. So every row here carries the class routing, the caveat that it excludes the in-building walk, and a direct link to the route used. If you would rather hold the stricter line, read the numbers as a rough gate and nothing more — the MGM Grand pair at 39 minutes fails under either reading, and that is the one result the two approaches agree on.

The honest summary: distance is measured, authority is not. No casino's own page will confirm these figures, and none is claimed to.

What the matrix says about where to stay

If the trip is Venetian-based (the property with the largest and most completely catalogued schedule), only three hotels pass the gate: The Venetian itself (7 min from its lobby to the poker room, which is actually in The Palazzo), The Palazzo (4 min), and Wynn (12 min). Caesars Palace passes at 19 minutes but has verified daily tournaments of its own, so the walk matters less. Bellagio sits at 28 minutes — technically inside the limit, practically marginal. MGM Grand fails outright at 39 minutes.

If the trip is Caesars Palace-based, the picture widens: Caesars Palace itself, Bellagio at 13 minutes, The Palazzo at 15 minutes, Wynn at 22 minutes and Horseshoe at 11 minutes all pass. That makes Caesars Palace the most walk-connected base on the Strip for tournament purposes — and it is also the property whose resort fee could not be verified.

If the trip is spread across properties, note that the Venetian→Caesars Palace walk is 15–19 minutes depending on direction, so a Venetian base can reach the Caesars Palace 10:00 AM daily and be back for a Venetian 5:10 PM bounty. That is the only genuinely two-event day the measurements support, and even then the morning event's finish time is unknown.
