Source hierarchy, and why it is not a formality

Two of the six venues publish no buy-in on their own site. Horseshoe's six daily start times exist only in a Caesars corporate blog post; the room's own flyer URLs return nothing. That is enough to list the times as editorial, and not enough to put a buy-in in a cell. The distinction between "official" and "official-editorial" is what keeps that gap visible instead of filling it with a plausible number.

The same rule applies to walking times. Google Maps is a routing service, not a casino policy: its pin for a resort is not the lobby door, and it does not know about the corridor from the Encore tower to the Wynn poker room. It is the best measurement available and it is labelled as a measurement, not a policy.

Structure sheets read, and not read

The Venetian links 37 structure PDFs from its DeepStack IV schedule. 14 were opened and read in full for this snapshot: sheets 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11, 12, 15, 18, 20, 21, 29, 34. Each of those was checked for the buy-in, the fee split, the starting stack, the level length, the late-registration close, and any language about re-entry, rebuys, an add-on or a maximum number of entries.

23 sheets were not opened. Their events appear with the buy-in, date and time from the schedule, and a blank fee and blank chips. That is why the ranking page ranks 148 events rather than all 94 Venetian rows: a ratio built on an unread sheet would be a guess.
