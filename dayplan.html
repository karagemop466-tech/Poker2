There is no all-day schedule, and here is exactly why

The brief asks for a schedule from 8 AM that maximises tournaments per vacation day. Two gaps in what the rooms publish block it, and neither is a gap in the research.

Nobody publishes a finish time. Not one room states when a tournament ends. The structure sheets give a starting stack, minutes per level and the level at which late registration closes — and stop there. So no second event can be scheduled with confidence: whether you are free at 5 PM depends on how long you survive, which is the one variable a schedule cannot control.

Nobody publishes an 8 AM start. The earliest start backed by a room's own document is 10:00 AM at Caesars Palace; the Venetian follows at 10:10 AM. A 9 AM Horseshoe slot exists only in a Caesars corporate article with no flyer behind it (F-07). Starting at 8 AM would mean two hours with nothing to enter.

This page does the honest version instead: one committed event per day, and a fallback labelled with the exact condition under which it becomes available. It is a plan you can follow rather than a grid that assumes you bust out on cue.

How the fallback test works

For a later event to count as a fallback, three things must be true — and all three are published:

  1. It starts later than the primary, per the room's own schedule.
  2. You can get there — the same building, or a property inside the measured 30-minute limit on the walking page.
  3. Its late registration is still open after you could plausibly be eliminated. This is the binding constraint, because late-registration closes are published and finish times are not.

A player who survives the primary cannot take the fallback, and every fallback row says so. It is insurance against an early exit, priced at the cost of a second bullet — not a second tournament squeezed into the same day.

An earlier draft of this page computed a "derived maximum length" per event from a table of level counts. That table was written from memory rather than transcribed from the sheets, so it was removed rather than corrected — see Repo review. No event-length figure appears anywhere on this site now.

What the sheets do publish, and what the dataset therefore holds: the starting stack, the minutes per level, and the point at which late registration closes. The $600 one-day sheet, for example, prints 28 levels of 30 minutes with seven 15-minute breaks and states "Late Registration: 10 levels + Break (~ 4:55 PM)". That last figure is the room's own approximate close time — the only clock time any structure sheet gives, and it is an approximation rather than a schedule.
